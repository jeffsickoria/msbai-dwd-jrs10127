HOW TO READ THE RESULTS

How does a county get its score?

Seven factors, one shared set of priorities, and a clear record of what is known.

The basic idea

  1. Use the same proposed center. Compare the same size and operating assumptions in all selected counties.
  2. Assess each factor on a 0–100 scale. Higher is more favorable. The rules for turning data into scores still need team review.
  3. Apply the priorities. A factor with a 25% weight contributes one quarter of its score to the total.
  4. Add the contributions. Rank the counties and show the gaps. A close second may still be preferred for reasons outside the model.

Example of the arithmetic only: an 80/100 factor with a 25% weight contributes 20 points. This is not a county finding.

What do the labels mean?

Available evidence: sources we have, with their review status shown. Overall scores are pending.

What-if assumptions: scores entered by an analyst to explore a question. They are not accepted findings.

Missing information, uncertainty and source changes

All seven factors must be comparable across all selected counties before we show an overall score. Missing factors are not zero and their weights are not moved to other factors.

Testing different priorities tells us whether the ranking depends on preferences. It does not establish how certain the data is. Missing-data bounds are mathematical extremes, not confidence intervals.

Sources are frozen snapshots. Historical demand remains separate from forecasts and from promised power at a particular site. The website does not automatically refresh the data.

JUL 1–DEC 30, 2025 · LOCAL TIME

Compare five scheduling strategies

Same evaluation window and daily energy within a profile. Bills include full modeled demand charges for partial local months. Hindsight comparators use realized demand; the causal policy assumes an eight-week history and a 24-hour publication embargo. True as-of vintages remain unverified.

What the top-100 measure tells us

It averages the facility’s added MW during the region’s 100 highest baseline-demand hours. Relief is the reduction from no intervention; a positive relief value means less added load at those hours. This differs from minimizing the single maximum combined peak.

All 32 retrospective policy schedules match a simple known-price daily scheduling rule within 0.000001 MW. Forecast information has not demonstrated additional scheduling value under these settings. The maximum-peak objective reaches the same mandatory-workload floor in every case; top-100 contributions can still differ. A maximum-peak optimizer does not optimize the top-100 mean.

    What data are we using?

    What we actually use and how

    Use status explains what feeds a screen or experiment. Review status explains its limitations. Files are frozen snapshots, not live API feeds; this website does not automatically refresh them. Unknown raw storage locations are stated explicitly.

    What do candidate, conditional and accepted mean?

    Candidate: identified or acquired, with checks still needed. Conditional: checked for a limited stated use, with qualifications or acceptance outstanding. Accepted: a reviewer has accepted a specific scope. None of these labels alone establishes suitability or available site power.
