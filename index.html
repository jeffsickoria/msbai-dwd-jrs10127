Acceptable departure windows

BTS departures from 2022–2024. Options need 100 classified flights across at least 12 months. The 95% intervals resample whole observed months and describe uncertainty in the historical rate. How the flight planner performed

Both rules chose from the same eligible options using 2022–2024 history. The planner ranked each airport and time window separately. The baseline ranked airports using their pooled all-day disruption rates for the destination. The test compares those fixed choices on 2025 outcomes.

Weekly demand review

Review weeks for unusually high or low total trips. Two or more consecutive flags in the same direction within a Monday–Sunday week form a sustained run. The member and casual gaps use each group’s own baseline and can differ in sum from the total gap.

How often would the rules trigger?

A chronological replay across complete weeks in 2025 used only earlier counts. It flags days outside the second-lowest and second-highest totals from up to 12 prior matching weekdays, with at least 10 available. The weekly dashboard above uses the saved weather-adjusted bands. The replay measures review workload, since confirmed incident labels are unavailable.

How the Citi Bike review performed

Replay 13 complete weeks from June 2 through August 31, 2025. Each day uses its prior matching weekdays to set the expected range. Compare immediate review after one flag with the dashboard rule that waits for two consecutive flags in the same direction.

The replay measures alert workload and timing using historical review signals. The follow-up counts later signals and cannot verify incidents. Download the daily results.

Weather model and holdout results

The original project modeled daily ridership using temperature, precipitation, snow, day of week, and month. On the final 179 days of model coverage, the overall mean absolute error was 18.0% lower than the seasonal-naive benchmark. The model underpredicted part of the recent growth period.
